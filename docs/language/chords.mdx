An instrument's line holds a `chords` value when the word `chords` comes first. Without it, the line is read as notes.

<Cell>

```composer-nb
play guitar: chords C - - G|Am - F G|%|F - _ -
```

</Cell>

## Charts

`|` separates bars. Inside a bar, the tokens split the bar into equal slots.

| Token | In its slot |
| --- | --- |
| A chord symbol | Starts the chord. |
| A Roman numeral | Starts the chord on that degree of the [`key`](/language/settings). |
| `-` | Holds the chord. |
| `_` | Silence. |
| `%` | Repeats the bar before. It stands alone in its bar. |

Each slot must be a whole number of sixteenth notes, so a `4 over 4` bar takes 1, 2, 4, 8 or 16 slots. Rhythm comes from the slots: `_ Am _ Am` plays on the off-beats.

A chart written as the same bars more than once is an error, because a pattern repeats it: `Am F|Am F` and `Am F|%` are `Am F`. Inside a bar, `C C` strikes the chord twice.

A long chart continues on the next line when that line starts with `|`.

## Chord symbols

A symbol is a root, a quality and an optional bass note: `C`, `Am`, `F#m`, `Bb7`, `Cmaj7`, `G7sus4`, `Em7b5`, `Cadd9`, `C/E`. No quality means major.

## Roman numerals

Uppercase is a major chord and lowercase a minor one, on that degree of the current [`key`](/language/settings#key). Degrees count on the major scale of the key's root, whatever the key's mode, as [degrees in notes](/language/notes#degrees) do. A `b` or `#` in front moves the root a semitone, so `bVII` is a whole step below the root in every key: under `set key Am` the natural minor's chords are `i bIII iv v bVI bVII`, and `II` in `E phrygian` is `bII`. The case gives the third, and a quality follows the numeral as it follows a chord's root: `V7`, `ii7`, `IVmaj7`, `viidim`, `viidim7`, `viim7b5`.

<Cell>

```composer-nb
set key C
play piano: chords I vi|IV V7|bVII IV|ii7 viidim
```

</Cell>

In C, this plays `C Am`, `F G7`, `Bb F`, `Dm7 Bdim`.

<Cell>

```composer-nb
set key Am
play piano: chords i bVI|bVII V7
```

</Cell>

In A minor, this plays `Am F`, `G E7`.

## Voicing

Guitars play the fretboard shape for each chord where one is known. [`transpose`](/language/settings#transpose-and-octave) moves the sound of any instrument's chords and keeps their names, so a guitar's shapes under `set transpose 2` sound as with a capo at the 2nd fret. Other instruments stack the chord's notes upward from the root in the current `octave`, with the bass note an octave below.
