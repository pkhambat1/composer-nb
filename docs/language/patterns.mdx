---
title: "Parts and composition"
description: "Lines, patterns, blocks and groups: how material is given to kit pieces and instruments and combined in time."
---

```text Syntax
part    ::= line
          | name
          | [length] ("pattern" | "block") body
          | length name
          | [length] group
          | every
          | placed
          | with_scope
line    ::= kit_piece ":" steps_expr
          | instrument ":" pitched
          | instrument ":" "{" pitched ("," pitched)* [","] "}"
pitched ::= "notes" note_seq | "chords" chart
body    ::= "{" entry ("," entry)* [","] "}"
entry   ::= part | declaration
group   ::= "(" part+ ")"
every   ::= "every" number "beats" (line | name | group | [length] body)
placed  ::= part "on" "bar" number ["to" number]
```

A part is anything that can be played. `steps`, `notes` and `chords` are not parts: they become one when a line gives them to a kit piece or an instrument. Parts are combined in two ways: side by side in a body, where they sound together, and in a row in a group, where they follow one another.

## Parts

| Form | Example | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Line](/language/patterns#lines) | `kick: x---` | One kit piece or instrument, and what it plays. |
| Name | `groove` | A declared `pattern` or `block`. |
| [pattern](/language/patterns#pattern) | `pattern { kick: x---, hat: x- }` | Entries that sound together. Its lines repeat. |
| [block](/language/patterns#block) | `block { crash: x }` | Entries that sound together. Its lines play once. |
| [Group](/language/patterns#groups) | `(verse chorus)` | Parts played one after another. |
| [Length prefix](/language/patterns#length-of-a-part) | `4 bars groove` | A pattern name or group repeated for that long, or an inline pattern or block of that length. |
| [every](/language/patterns#every) | `every 1/2 beats hat: x-` | A part whose steps have another length. |
| [Placed part](/language/patterns#on-bar) | `fill on bar 4` | A part that starts at a bar. |

Any part may also be given local settings by wrapping it in `with`. That is a scope, not a kind of composition, and it is defined in [with](/language/scope#with).

A part goes after `play`, in a body, in a group, or after `=` in a `pattern` or `block` declaration.

## Lines

```text Syntax
line ::= kit_piece ":" steps_expr
       | instrument ":" "notes" note_seq
       | instrument ":" "chords" chart
       | instrument ":" "{" pitched ("," pitched)* [","] "}"
```

A line says what plays and what it plays. The colon joins the two; it is not a type annotation.

| Operand | Type | May be |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `kit_piece` | `kit_piece` | `kick`, `snare`, `snare.ghost`, `snare.rim`, `snare.cross`, `hat`, `hat.open`, `hat.pedal`, `ride`, `ride.bell`, `crash`, `tom.high`, `tom.low`, `tom.floor`. See [Kit pieces](/drums/kit#kit-pieces). |
| `steps_expr` | `steps` | Step words (`x--- --x-`), a `steps` name, repeats (`4*x-`), a Euclidean rhythm (`3 in 8`), a fill (`...-`), or layers (`{ x---, --^- }`). See [steps](/drums/steps). |
| `instrument` | `instrument` | `piano`, `piano.electric`, `organ`, `bass`, `guitar`, `guitar.electric`. See [Instruments](/pitched/instruments#instruments). |
| `note_seq` | `notes` | Note words (`C4--- 1-3-`), a `notes` name, repeats, interval calls, a fill. See [notes](/pitched/notes). |
| `chart` | `chords` | Chord symbols and degrees in bars (`C G \\| Am F`), a `chords` name, interval calls. See [chords](/pitched/chords). |
| `pitched` | `notes` or `chords` | `"notes" note_seq` or `"chords" chart`: one voice of an instrument's [layers](/pitched/instruments#layers). |

A kit piece takes `steps` and nothing else, so its line has no type keyword. An instrument takes `notes` or `chords`, so its line always says which, before a name as well.

A line stands in a body, or directly after `play`. In a group, a line must be on a line of its own.

```composer-nb Example
steps pulse = x--x--x-
notes walk = 1-3-5-3-
play pattern {
  kick: pulse,
  hat: x-,
  bass: notes walk,
  piano: chords C G,
}
```

```composer-nb Invalid
play pattern { piano: 1---3--- }
```

```text Error
piano takes notes or chords, and says which: piano: notes 1---3---
```

```composer-nb Invalid
steps pulse = x--x--x-
play pattern { piano: notes pulse }
```

```text Error
pulse is steps, so it goes on a drum's line, not on piano's
```

## Bodies

```text Syntax
body  ::= "{" entry ("," entry)* [","] "}"
entry ::= part | declaration
```

| Operand | Type | May be |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `entry` | `part` | Any [part](/language/patterns#parts): a line, a name, an inline pattern or block, a group, an `every`, a placed part. |
| `entry` | declaration | A [declaration](/language/values#declarations) of any type. The name is local to the body. |

A body is the braces of a pattern, a block, an `every` or a `with`. Its parts start together. Entries are separated by commas, on one line or many, and the last may be followed by a comma.

- A body must contain at least one part. Declarations alone play nothing.
- A kit piece or instrument may have one line directly in a body. Several figures for one kit piece are [steps layers](/drums/steps#layers); several voices for one instrument are [layers](/pitched/instruments#layers). A name or nested body may add more events for the same kit piece or instrument.
- `play`, `print` and `set` are statements, not entries.

Braces written on their own always carry their type: `pattern { ... }` or `block { ... }`. After `=` in a declaration the type is already given, and after `every` or `with` the body is a pattern; in those three places the braces take no keyword.

```composer-nb Invalid
play pattern { kick: x---, kick: --x- }
```

```text Error
kick already has a line in this pattern. To layer it, put its lines in a block: kick: { ... }
```

```composer-nb Invalid
play pattern { steps pulse = x--x--x- }
```

```text Error
Nothing to play in this pattern
```

## pattern

```text Syntax
"pattern" name "=" ([length] body | part)
[length] "pattern" body
```

A `pattern` is a body whose lines repeat. Every entry starts at the beginning of the pattern:

| Entry | In a pattern |
| --- | --- |
| Line | Repeats, each at its own length. |
| Pattern (name or inline), group, `every`, `with` | Repeats to the end of the pattern. |
| Block (name or inline) | Plays once. |
| Placed part | Plays in its bars; see [on bar](/language/patterns#on-bar). |

Lines need not be the same length. Without a `length`, the pattern lasts until its repeating entries realign, and at least as long as its blocks and placed parts. With a `length`, every repeating entry must fit it a whole number of times.

```composer-nb Example
pattern groove = {
  kick: x--- --x-,
  snare: ---- x---,
  hat: x-,
}
play groove for 2 bars
```

Because a pattern's lines repeat, a line that writes one figure out several times is an error; see [In a pattern, write the figure once](/drums/steps#in-a-pattern-write-the-figure-once).

## block

```text Syntax
"block" name "=" ([length] body | part)
[length] "block" body
```

A `block` is a body whose lines play once. Every entry starts at the beginning of the block:

| Entry | In a block |
| --- | --- |
| Line | Plays once. |
| Pattern (name or inline), group, `every`, `with` | Repeats to the end of the block. |
| Block (name or inline) | Plays once. |
| Placed part | Plays in its bars. |

Without a `length`, the block lasts as long as its longest entry, and its repeating entries must fit that a whole number of times. A block is one occurrence wherever it is used: inside a pattern it sounds once and is silent after, and it takes no length prefix.

```composer-nb Example
block ending = { crash: x, kick: x, piano: chords C }
play ending
```

The only difference between the two types is their lines: `pattern { kick: x--- }` plays that bar over and over, and `block { kick: x--- }` plays it once.

## Groups

```text Syntax
group ::= "(" part+ ")"
```

| Operand | Type | May be |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `part` | `part` | A name, an inline pattern or block, a nested group, a `with`, a part with a length prefix, a placed part. A line, if it is on a line of its own. |

A group plays its parts one after another. It lasts as long as its parts together. Parts are separated by spaces or newlines, not commas.

Inside a pattern or block, or under a length prefix, a group repeats as a whole; a block in it still sounds only once. Parts that are meant to follow one another must be in a group: two names side by side without parentheses are an error.

A group is the place to change `time`, `tempo` or `swing` part way through, because its parts do not sound together; see [with](/language/scope#with).

```composer-nb Example
pattern verse = { kick: x--- --x-, hat: x- }
block fill = { snare: xxxx xxxx xxxx xxxx }
play (3 bars verse fill)
```

```composer-nb Invalid
pattern verse = { kick: x--- --x-, hat: x- }
play verse verse
```

```text Error
What plays in order goes in brackets: (verse verse)
```

## Length of a part

| Part | Lasts |
| --- | --- |
| Line | The length of its `steps`, `notes` or `chords`. |
| Pattern without a length | Until its repeating entries realign. This may be part of a bar: lines of 4 and 6 steps realign after 12. |
| Block without a length | As long as its longest entry. |
| Group | The sum of its parts. |
| `length "pattern" body`, `length "block" body` | `length`. |
| `length name`, `length group` | `length`. The pattern or group repeats and must fit a whole number of times. Not allowed before a block name. |
| Placed part | From the start of the surrounding part to the end of its last bar. |

A line that ends in a `...` fill has no length and does not count: some other entry, or an explicit length, must set the end. Lines that would not realign within 64 bars need an explicit length.

A length is a property of the part where it is written. To set how long an output lasts, use `for` on [play](/language/play#duration).

## every

```text Syntax
every ::= "every" number "beats" (line | name | group | [length] body)
```

| Operand | Type | May be |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `number` | `number` | The length of one step in beats (quarter notes). At least 1/12, and a whole number of ticks at 96 per beat. |
| Operand | `part` | A line, a `pattern` or `block` name, a group, or a body. The body takes no keyword and is a pattern. |

Sets how long one step lasts inside its part. The default is 1/4 beat, a sixteenth note. The value is absolute, not relative to an enclosing `every`, and an `every` that sets the length already in effect is an error.

| `every` | One step is |
| --- | --- |
| `every 1/2 beats` | An eighth note. |
| `every 1/3 beats` | An eighth-note triplet. |
| `every 1/6 beats` | A sixteenth-note triplet. |
| `every 1/8 beats` | A thirty-second note. |
| `every 3/4 beats` | A dotted eighth note. |
| `every 1 beats` | A quarter note. |

`every` changes `steps` and `notes`. It does not change `chords`, whose slots divide bars. Lines outside the `every` keep their own step length.

```composer-nb Example
play pattern {
  kick: x--- --x-,
  every 1/3 beats hat: x-x,
  every 1 beats bass: notes 1 5,
}
```

```composer-nb Invalid
play every 1/2 steps kick: x-
```

```text Error
every sets a step's length in beats: every 1/2 beats { kick: x }
```

## on bar

```text Syntax
placed ::= part "on" "bar" first ["to" last]
```

| Operand | Type | May be |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `part` | `part` | A line, a name, an inline pattern or block, a group, an `every`, a `with`. |
| `first`, `last` | `number` | Whole numbers from 1 to 64, `last` not less than `first`. Arithmetic is allowed. |

Starts a part at the beginning of bar `first` of the surrounding part, counting from 1, and gives it the bars up to and including `last`, or `first` alone. A pattern repeats through its bars. A block plays once in them.

`on bar` comes after the part it places. A placed part adds to what is already sounding; it does not silence other lines. In a pattern, it plays in those bars on every pass.

```composer-nb Example
block fill = { snare: xxxx xxxx xxxx xxxx }
play pattern {
  kick: x--- --x-,
  hat: x-,
  fill on bar 4,
  crash: x on bar 1,
}
```

```composer-nb Invalid
play pattern { hat: x-, kick: x--- on bar 3 to 2 }
```

```text Error
bar 3 to 2 runs backwards. The first bar comes first
```

## Parts that overlap

Parts in a body sound together, so two of them may play the same kit piece or instrument at the same moment.

| Overlap | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Two hits on one kit piece at the same time | The one written later is kept. Silence never removes a hit. |
| Two notes in one voice of an instrument at the same time | The one written later is kept. |
| Voices in an instrument's layers | Independent: all of them sound. |
| `hat.open` and `hat` at the same time | The open hit is kept. |
