`play` evaluates what follows it and renders one output. It is the only statement that makes sound, and it goes at the top level of a cell.

```text
play [<length>] <pattern, sequence, block or line>
```

<Cell>

```composer-nb
pattern groove = {
  kick: x---x---
  snare: ----x---
}

play groove
play 2 bars piano: chords Am F
play 4 bars {
  crash: 1
  groove
}
```

</Cell>

This cell renders three outputs.

## What repeats

A play is a timeline, and its statements start together.

| In a play | Plays |
| --- | --- |
| A line | Once. |
| A single pattern | Repeated until the play ends. |
| A sequence of two or more | In order, once. |
| `every` | Repeated until the play ends. |
| `bar <n>` | In bar `n`. |

## Duration

Without a length, a play lasts as long as its longest statement, and what repeats must fit that a whole number of times. With a length, it lasts exactly that long: whatever is repeating stops at the end, and silence fills any time left over. A play can't run past 10 minutes.

## Output

- A header with the time, tempo, number of bars and the instruments that play.
- Audio with a waveform, a WAV download, and a MIDI file for a DAW or a notation program.
- A grid of every hit and note, bar by bar. The playground can draw it as sheet music instead: the kit on a percussion staff with hands up and feet down, each instrument on a staff of its own (a guitar with its tab), and the sheet prints.
- The chords played. The playground draws a diagram for each.
