A [chord symbol](/pitched/chords#chord-symbols) is a root, a suffix and an optional bass. This page lists every suffix the language knows by name. Suffixes outside this list can be [composed with modifiers](/pitched/chords#composing-a-suffix).

## Reading the catalog

Each row is one chord quality. Its suffixes are aliases: they sound identical. Suffixes are case-sensitive. The intervals are counted from the root against the major scale: `3` is a major third, `b3` a minor third, `#5` an augmented fifth, `bb7` a diminished seventh. `9`, `11` and `13` sound an octave above `2`, `4` and `6`. The list is exactly what sounds, so a quality that omits its fifth shows no `5`.

After a degree, a suffix cannot start with a digit; see [Chords by degree](/pitched/chords#chords-by-degree). The preferred spellings below work after both letters and degrees unless noted.

## Spelling restrictions

- A 6/9 chord is `6/9` or `m6/9`. `69`, `m69`, `6add9` and `maj6/9` are errors.
- After a degree, `6/9` alone would read as a bass: `6/9` is the chord on degree 6 over degree 9. Write `1M6/9`.
- `b9sus` cannot follow a letter root directly: `Cb9sus` is C flat with the suffix `9sus`. For C with a flat ninth and a suspended fourth, write `C7b9sus4`.
- `°` and `ø` are accepted after a letter root only.
- An interval function can produce tones that match no row here. `print` then shows the tones without a chord name.
