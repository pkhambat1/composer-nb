An [instrument](/pitched/instruments)'s line says which type it holds: `chords` in front of a chart, or [`notes`](/pitched/notes) in front of notes. A line without one of them is an error.

<Cell>

```composer-nb
play guitar: chords C - - G|Am - F G|%|F - _ -
```

</Cell>

## Charts

`|` separates bars. Inside a bar, the tokens split the bar into equal slots.

| Token | In its slot |
| --- | --- |
| A chord symbol | Starts the chord. |
| A Roman numeral | Starts the chord on that degree of the [`key`](/pitched/key). |
| A numeral `of` a numeral | Starts the chord on that degree of another chord's root: [`V7 of V`](#counting-from-a-chord). |
| `-` | Holds the chord. |
| `_` | Silence. |
| `%` | Repeats the bar before. It stands alone in its bar. |

Each slot must be a whole number of sixteenth notes, so a `4 over 4` bar takes 1, 2, 4, 8 or 16 slots. Rhythm comes from the slots: `_ Am _ Am` plays on the off-beats.

A chart written as the same bars more than once is an error, because a pattern repeats it: `Am F|Am F` and `Am F|%` are `Am F`. Inside a bar, `C C` strikes the chord twice.

A long chart continues on the next line when that line starts with `|`.

## Chord symbols

A symbol is a root, a quality and an optional bass note: `C`, `Am`, `F#m`, `Bb7`, `Cmaj7`, `G7sus4`, `Em7b5`, `Cadd9`, `C6/9`, `C/E`. No quality means major.

A 6/9 chord is written as on a lead sheet, `C6/9` or `Cm6/9`. Its `/` is part of the chord, so `C6/9/E` is C6/9 over E. `C69`, `C6add9` and `Cmaj6/9` are errors.

## Roman numerals

Uppercase is a major chord and lowercase a minor one, on that degree of the current [`key`](/pitched/key). A plain lowercase numeral takes the scale's own fifth too, so it is diminished where the scale makes it so: `vii` in a major key, `ii` in minor, `iv` in lydian. Write `dim`, or `m7b5` and the like, to say it outright. Degrees count on the key's own scale, its mode's, as [degrees in notes](/pitched/notes#degrees) do: in A minor `III` is C and `VII` is G, and in A major they're C# and G#. A `b` or `#` in front moves the root a semitone off the scale: `bVII` in C major is Bb. The case gives the third, and a quality follows the numeral as it follows a chord's root: `V7`, `ii7`, `IVmaj7`, `viidim`, `viidim7`, `viim7b5`.

<Cell>

```composer-nb
play piano: chords I vi|IV V7|bVII IV|ii7 viidim
```

</Cell>

In C, this plays `C Am`, `F G7`, `Bb F`, `Dm7 Bdim`.

<Cell>

```composer-nb
set key A
set mode minor
play piano: chords i ii|III iv|v VI|VII i
```

</Cell>

In A minor, this plays every chord of the mode in turn: `Am Bdim`, `C Dm`, `Em F`, `G Am`.

## Digits

A digit, `1` to `7`, is the chord the key's scale has on that degree, so a chart follows the mode: `chords 1 2|3 4|5 6|7 1` plays D Em F#m G A Bm C#dim D in D major, Dm Edim F Gm Am Bb C Dm in D minor, and Dm Em F G Am Bdim C Dm in D dorian. Use a numeral when the chord should be the same whatever the mode, and a digit when the mode should decide.

## Counting from a chord

`of` counts a numeral from another chord's root instead of from the key's: `V7 of V` is the `V7` built on the key's `V`, a secondary dominant. The numeral counts on the major scale of that root, so in C, `V7 of V` is `D7` and `ii7 of IV` is `Gm7`. After `of` goes a numeral of the key. Only its root counts, so `V of v` is the same as `V of V`. A bass note goes at the end: `V7 of V/F#`.

To count from a chord that is itself counted with `of`, put it in brackets, as with notes. `V7 of (V of V)` is the `V7` of D, which is `A7`.

<Cell>

```composer-nb
play piano: chords V7 of (V of V)|V7 of V|V7|I
```

</Cell>

In C, this plays `A7`, `D7`, `G7`, `C`.

## Voicing

Guitars play the fretboard shape for each chord where one is known. [`transpose`](/pitched/instruments#transpose) moves the sound of any instrument's chords and keeps their names, so a guitar's shapes under `set transpose 2` sound as with a capo at the 2nd fret. Other instruments stack the chord's notes upward from its root, with the bass note an octave below. A numeral's root counts up from the key's root in the current `octave`, as a [degree](/pitched/notes#degrees) does, so a progression keeps its shape in every key: `I vi IV V` is C3 A3 F3 G3 in C and E3 C#4 A3 B3 in E. A chord symbol's root is in the current `octave`, counted from C. A numeral `of` another one counts up from where that chord's root sounds, so in A, `V7 of V` is B4, a 5th above V's E4. A bass note after `/` goes in the octave below, counted up from the key's root for a numeral and from C for a symbol.
