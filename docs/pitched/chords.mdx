An [instrument](/pitched/instruments)'s line says which type it holds: `chords` in front of a chart, or [`notes`](/pitched/notes) in front of notes. A line without one of them is an error.

```composer-nb
play guitar: chords C - - G|Am - F G|%|F - _ -
```

## Charts

`|` separates bars. Inside a bar, the tokens split the bar into equal slots.

| Token | In its slot |
| --- | --- |
| A chord symbol | Starts the chord. |
| A number `1` to `7` | Starts the chord on that degree of the [`key`](/pitched/key). |
| `-` | Holds the chord. |
| `_` | Silence. |
| `%` | Repeats the bar before. It stands alone in its bar. |

Each slot must be a whole number of sixteenth notes, so a `4 over 4` bar takes 1, 2, 4, 8 or 16 slots. Rhythm comes from the slots: `_ Am _ Am` plays on the off-beats.

A chart written as the same bars more than once is an error, because a pattern repeats it: `Am F|Am F` and `Am F|%` are `Am F`. Inside a bar, `C C` strikes the chord twice.

A long chart continues on the next line when that line starts with `|`.

A name holding one chord behaves like that chord written directly: after `chords home = 1m7`, `home fifth(home, -1)` is two slots in one bar. Names and interval functions do not add bar lines around single chords. Write `home | fifth(home, -1)` for two bars.

A name holding several chords or explicit bar lines is a complete chart. Joining named charts preserves their bars: `verse chorus` does not merge the last bar of `verse` with the first bar of `chorus`. To make a single chord an explicit full-bar chart, include its boundaries, as in `chords homeBar = |1m7|`.

## Chord symbols

A symbol is a root, a quality and an optional bass note: `C`, `Am`, `F#m`, `Bb7`, `Cmaj7`, `G7sus4`, `Em7b5`, `Cadd9`, `C6/9`, `C/E`. No quality means major.

A 6/9 chord is written as on a lead sheet, `C6/9` or `Cm6/9`. Its `/` is part of the chord, so `C6/9/E` is C6/9 over E. `C69`, `C6add9` and `Cmaj6/9` are errors.

## Numeric degrees

A positive integer names a chord on the key's scale. In C major, `1 2 3 4 5 6 7` means C Dm Em F G Am Bdim. The mode supplies the quality. Degrees continue into higher octaves: `8` is C one octave up, `15` is C two octaves up, and `17` is E two octaves up. In a six-note whole-tone scale, `7` and `13` are the next two tonics instead. Lowercase Roman numerals remain accepted for existing scores.

Negative numbers count down through the same scale, skipping zero. In C major, `-1` is B below C, `-2` is A, and `-7` is the lower C. Thus `-1sus2` is Bsus2 below the tonic; `-7sus2` is Csus2 an octave down. In `wholeTone`, `-6` is the lower tonic. The same signs work in slash basses and before accidentals: `1/-1`, `-b1sus2`. A standalone `-` still holds the previous chord.

**All consecutive digits belong to the root degree.** An extension therefore starts with a quality or modifier: `1maj7`, `2m7`, `5dom7`, `1maj9`, `1M6/9`. `17` is degree seventeen, not degree one with a seventh; `1 7` is two chords. `1sus2add11#5/4` has degree 1, a suspension, additions and alterations, then a bass degree.

`b` or `#` before the root moves it one semitone off the scale and defaults to a major chord: `b7` is Bb in C major. An explicit quality overrides the scale: `5m`, `5M`, `7dim`, `4maj7`. Additions and alterations without an explicit quality modify the scale's triad: `2add11` is Dm with G added in C major. Use `octave(1)` to move up and `octave(1, -1)` to move down. Apostrophe notation is no longer accepted.

```composer-nb
chords chart = 1 2|3 4|5 6|7 8

with key D, mode dorian { play piano: chords chart, }
with key D, mode minor { play piano: chords chart, }
with key D, mode lydian { play piano: chords chart, }
```

### Composing a chord suffix

A suffix can combine a quality, suspension, additions and alterations. `sus2` replaces the third with a second; `sus4` (or `sus`) replaces it with a fourth. `add11` adds the eleventh without implying a seventh or ninth. `#5` replaces the fifth with a raised fifth. Additions may include an accidental: `add#11`.

A slash ends the chord and specifies its bass. A number after `/` is a degree of the active key, including its mode, rather than a chord interval. A letter names the bass absolutely. In C major:

| Written | Tones | Bass |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `1sus2add11#5/4` | C D G# F | F |
| `2add11` | D F A G | D |
| `5dom7/b7` | G B D F | Bb |
| `1M6/9/3` | C E G A D | E |

In `1M6/9/3`, `1` is the root, `M6/9` is the major 6/9 quality, and `/3` is the bass. `6/9` alone means degree 6 over degree 9; the slash is a bass separator unless the suffix has already begun.

```composer-nb
play piano: chords 1sus2add11#5/4 2add11|5dom7/b7 1M6/9/3
```

## Interval functions

`fifth(value)` moves **every tone**, including the bass, four positions up the active scale. A value can be one chord, an inline chart, or a named chart. Rhythm, holds, rests and bar lines stay in place. Chord quality follows the resulting tones: in C major, `fifth(3)` moves E G B to B D F and becomes Bdim.

Use `second`, `third`, `fourth`, `fifth`, `sixth`, `seventh`, `octave`, or compound intervals `ninth` through `fifteenth`. `unison` is the identity. The optional second argument is a signed integer count: `fifth(1, 2)` applies the fifth twice, and `fifth(1, -1)` moves down a fifth. Counts range from -64 to 64; zero leaves the pitches alone. Calls can nest: `third(fifth(1))`.

```composer-nb
chords phrase = 1 3|4 5
chords answer = fifth(phrase)

play piano: chords phrase answer
with mode minor { play piano: chords answer, }
```

These are scale intervals: in C major, the fifth above B is F. Repeated fifths therefore stay in the active scale; they do not produce a chromatic circle of twelve perfect fifths. Chromatic offsets are carried from each original scale degree to its destination. Named results retain these rules and resolve under the key and mode where they play. [Notes](/pitched/notes#interval-functions) use the same functions.

## Voicing

Guitars play the fretboard shape for each chord where one is known. [`transpose`](/pitched/instruments#transpose) moves the sound of any instrument's chords and keeps their names, so a guitar's shapes under `set transpose 2` sound as with a capo at the 2nd fret. Other instruments stack the chord's notes upward from its root. A chord over a bass, such as `C/E`, adds that bass note below them; a plain chord has no separate bass note. A numeral's root counts up from the key's root in the current `octave`, as a [degree](/pitched/notes#degrees) does, so a progression keeps its shape in every key: `1 6 4 5` is C3 A3 F3 G3 in C and E3 C#4 A3 B3 in E. A chord symbol's root is in the current `octave`, counted from C. A bass note after `/` goes in the octave below, counted up from the key's root for a numeral and from C for a symbol.
