---
title: "Key and mode"
description: "How `key` and `mode` turn scale degrees into pitches, chords and written notes."
---

`key` gives the tonic and `mode` gives the scale. Together they decide what degrees in [notes](/pitched/notes#pitches) and [chords](/pitched/chords#chords-by-degree) mean, and where [interval functions](/functions/intervals) move. Notes and chords written with letter names are not affected.

## Modes

Each mode is a list of semitone distances above the tonic, one for each degree.

| Mode | Semitones above the tonic | Degrees per octave |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `major` | `0 2 4 5 7 9 11` | 7 |
| `minor` | `0 2 3 5 7 8 10` | 7 |
| `dorian` | `0 2 3 5 7 9 10` | 7 |
| `phrygian` | `0 1 3 5 7 8 10` | 7 |
| `lydian` | `0 2 4 6 7 9 11` | 7 |
| `mixolydian` | `0 2 4 5 7 9 10` | 7 |
| `locrian` | `0 1 3 5 6 8 10` | 7 |
| `harmonicMinor` | `0 2 3 5 7 8 11` | 7 |
| `melodicMinor` | `0 2 3 5 7 9 11` | 7 |
| `wholeTone` | `0 2 4 6 8 10` | 6 |

`minor` is natural minor. `melodicMinor` is the ascending form in both directions.

## Degrees

```text Syntax
degree ::= ["-"] ["b" | "#"] digit+
```

Degree `1` is the tonic in the octave in effect. Positive degrees climb the scale and continue past the octave. Negative degrees descend from the tonic; there is no degree 0. `b` or `#` lowers or raises the resulting pitch by a semitone, and the minus sign comes first: `-b1`.

| Pitch | Seven-degree modes | `wholeTone` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tonic | `1` | `1` |
| Tonic one octave up | `8` | `7` |
| Tonic two octaves up | `15` | `13` |
| Scale tone below the tonic | `-1` | `-1` |
| Tonic one octave down | `-7` | `-6` |

A degree is a whole number from -128 to 128, and the pitch it names must be within MIDI 0 to 127.

```composer-nb Example
print(notes 1 3 5 8 -1 -7 b3)
```

```text Output
notes 1(C3) 3(E3) 5(G3) 8(C4) -1(B2) -7(C2) b3(Eb3)
```

```composer-nb Example
with key A, mode minor { print(notes 1 3 5 8 -1 -7 #7) }
```

```text Output
notes 1(A3) 3(C4) 5(E4) 8(A4) -1(G3) -7(A2) #7(G#4)
```

In `notes`, a degree is one pitch. In `chords`, a degree is the triad the scale builds on that pitch: every other scale tone, three of them. A suffix replaces or alters that triad; see [Chords by degree](/pitched/chords#chords-by-degree).

```composer-nb Example
print(chords 1 2 3 4 5 6 7)
```

```text Output
chords 1(C(C3 E3 G3)) 2(Dm(D3 F3 A3)) 3(Em(E3 G3 B3)) 4(F(F3 A3 C4)) 5(G(G3 B3 D4)) 6(Am(A3 C4 E4)) 7(Bdim(B3 D4 F4))
```

```composer-nb Example
with mode minor { print(chords 1 2 3 4 5 6 7) }
```

```text Output
chords 1(Cm(C3 Eb3 G3)) 2(Ddim(D3 F3 Ab3)) 3(Eb(Eb3 G3 Bb3)) 4(Fm(F3 Ab3 C4)) 5(Gm(G3 Bb3 D4)) 6(Ab(Ab3 C4 Eb4)) 7(Bb(Bb3 D4 F4))
```

## Spelling and key signature

Degrees are written with the letters of the scale, one letter per degree. An accidental on a degree is relative to the scale: `#7` in `minor` is the raised seventh. `wholeTone` has six degrees for seven letters, so its sixth degree takes the seventh letter: D whole tone is D E F# G# A# C.

The mode sets the key signature on the sheet. `harmonicMinor` and `melodicMinor` use the signature of `minor`, and `wholeTone` uses the signature of `major`; their other notes are written with accidentals.

## Named values and the key

A named `notes` or `chords` value keeps its degrees as degrees. It is resolved against the key, mode and octave in effect where it is played or printed, not where it was declared.

```composer-nb Example
notes riff = 1 3 5
with key D { print(riff) }
with key F, mode minor { print(riff) }
```

```text Output
notes 1(D3) 3(F#3) 5(A3)
notes 1(F3) 3(Ab3) 5(C4)
```
