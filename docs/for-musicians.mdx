You write what plays, one line per kit piece or instrument, and press **Play**. A bar of 4/4 is sixteen steps; `x` is a hit and `-` is nothing. A pattern repeats each line, so a line is written once at its shortest: `hat: x-` is eighth notes for as long as the pattern plays. Lines of different lengths drift against each other until they meet again, which is how a polyrhythm is written: just the lines.

<Cell>

```composer-nb
set tempo 100

pattern beat = {
  hat: x-
  snare: ---- x---
  kick: x--- --x- x--- ----
}

play 4 bars beat
```

</Cell>

**Hear it.** Play renders the audio and plays it. Run a cell again while it plays and it starts over from the top.

**See it.** Bricks: a row per kit piece, a cell per step, counted `1 e & a`. Or sheet music: the kit on a percussion staff with the hands up and the feet down, each instrument on a staff of its own, a guitar with its tab, with counts.

**Keep it.** A notebook is saved on your device as you write, and the sidebar lists them; the notebook's name opens its menu, to rename, duplicate or delete one. Print makes a PDF of the sheet.

**Write more.** The reference has three parts. [Language](/language/overview) is what every cell has: settings, patterns and [play](/language/play). [Drums](/drums/kit) is the kit: its pieces, and [steps](/drums/steps). [Pitched instruments](/pitched/instruments) is everything with a pitch: the [key](/pitched/key), [notes](/pitched/notes) and [chords](/pitched/chords).
