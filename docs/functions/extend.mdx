---
title: "extend"
description: "Apply one chord quality or modifier to every chord in a chart."
---

```text Syntax
extend(chart: chords, modifier: suffix) -> chords
```

| Parameter | Type | May be |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `chart` | `chords` | A chord, an inline chart, a `chords` name, or another chord call. |
| `modifier` | `suffix` | One unquoted chord suffix, such as `maj7`, `m7`, `dom7`, `add9`, `sus4` or `#11`. A composed suffix such as `maj7add9` is also accepted. |

The first argument is always the music; the second is always the suffix. The suffix applies to every chord. There is no list of modifiers and no pairing of two lists. `extend` returns a new `chords` value without changing its input or playing audio.

```composer-nb Example
set key D
set mode dorian
chords vamp = 1 | 4
chords richer = extend(vamp, add9)
play piano: chords richer
```

This keeps the D minor and G major triads and adds E and A respectively, giving Dm(add9) and G(add9).

## Chords only

`extend` accepts and returns `chords`. It does not convert a `notes` list. Bare roots in its first argument are read as chords, including when `print` supplies no type:

```composer-nb Example
chords roots = 1 2 | 5 1
print(extend(roots, maj7))
print(extend(C D | G C, maj7))
```

Both examples produce Cmaj7–Dmaj7–Gmaj7–Cmaj7 in the default key. To extend a sequence of roots, declare it as `chords` from the start.

## Quality versus modifier

| Suffix | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| `maj7`, `m7`, `dom7`, or another [catalog quality](/pitched/chord-symbols) | Replaces the existing quality, keeping the root and slash bass. `extend(2m7, maj7)` is Dmaj7 in C major. |
| `add9`, `add#11`, `b9`, `#5`, or another addition or alteration | Modifies the existing chord tones. `extend(2m7, add9)` keeps the minor third and seventh and adds the ninth. |
| `sus2`, `sus4`, `sus` | Removes any existing second, third and fourth, then adds a major second (`sus2`) or perfect fourth (`sus4`, `sus`). Other intervals stay unchanged, including compound intervals such as the ninth and eleventh. |
| `maj7add9`, `sus4add9`, or another composed suffix | Applies the quality, if present, then the modifiers from left to right. |

Additions and alterations count major or perfect intervals from each chord's root, independently of the key. They do not imply intermediate extensions: `add9` adds a ninth without adding a seventh. A later modifier of the same interval replaces an earlier one.

## Timing, settings and nesting

Bar lines, holds, rests and each chord's slot remain in place. `%` repeats the preceding bar inside the argument. Root registers and explicit slash basses stay unchanged. Named charts keep their existing bar boundaries, just as they do in an interval call.

Degrees remain symbolic and resolve in the key, mode and octave where the result is used. An explicit quality such as `maj7` overrides the mode's triad; `add9` preserves it. Every resulting tone and bass must fit MIDI 0 to 127. Playback `transpose` applies after the function.

Nest calls to apply successive changes:

```composer-nb Example
chords vamp = 1m7 | 4dom7
play piano: chords extend(extend(vamp, add9), #11)
```

Calls apply from the inside out, up to 32 levels. Order matters when combining `extend` with an [interval function](/functions/intervals): the interval moves every tone through the scale, while an explicit quality sets the tones above the resulting root.

```composer-nb Example
print(second(extend(1, maj7)))
print(extend(second(1), maj7))
```

In C major the first result is Dm7, and the second is Dmaj7. Functions cannot be declared or passed as values.

## Errors

```composer-nb Invalid
notes melody = C4 D4 G4 C5
print(extend(melody, maj7))
```

```composer-nb Invalid
print(extend(1 4, maj7, add9))
```

Use the single suffix `maj7add9`, or nest calls, instead of passing extra arguments.
